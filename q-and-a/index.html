Q&A

Questions and answers

Straight answers about how private-party appraisals work in El Cajon and across San Diego County.

Where does Brian inspect homes in person?

Inspected work (Drive-By and Standard reports, including 2-4 unit Standards) happens only in the 68 communities Brian Ward Appraisal covers in person: 33 in San Diego County, El Cajon included, and 35 in Riverside County. A property outside that list, whether in the San Diego backcountry, Los Angeles or the Bay Area, is handled with a desktop report. Each town page on this site says which applies.

Why does the order form ask about an effective date?

Many private-party assignments need the value as of a specific past day: the date of death for an estate, the petition date in a bankruptcy, a separation date in a divorce. The form asks whether a particular date applies and, if so, which one, so the quote reflects the right research period from the start. If nobody is sure yet, choose Not sure and Brian will help sort it out.

How long does an appraisal take?

The written quote for an El Cajon home states a firm delivery date before any work begins, and Brian plans the schedule around it. Retrospective dates, large rural parcels and properties with unusual improvements can take longer because the comparable sales are harder to locate and verify. If a hearing or filing is scheduled, mention it with the request so the delivery date can be set around that deadline.

Is Brian able to value a home as of an earlier date?

Yes. A big portion of his work is retrospective, most often for estates needing a stepped-up basis at the date of death and for divorces keyed to the date of separation. The analysis uses sales that closed around that earlier date, along with records and family recollections about how the home looked at the time. Dates going back many years are possible when enough information survives.

What is a desktop appraisal?

A desktop appraisal is prepared without visiting the home. Brian works from county records, permit files, prior listing photos, aerial imagery and photos or notes the owner provides, then applies the same sales comparison analysis used in any other report. It is still a signed appraisal by a certified appraiser. It works best when the property's condition is clear from the available information and no party is likely to challenge it.

Will a court accept the report?

Brian prepares his reports to the Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice, and a California certified residential appraiser signs each one, which matches what courts handling probate, family and civil matters generally expect. Whether a particular judge gives weight to a report depends on the case, so attorneys sometimes ask for an inspected report or added detail in contested matters. Brian can discuss those needs before the quote is written.

Can an attorney order the appraisal for a client?

Attorneys order a large share of Brian's work. The law firm can be named as the client, which keeps the engagement under counsel's direction and the report addressed to the firm. Fees can be invoiced to the firm or the estate. Brian is used to coordinating access with heirs, tenants or a spouse who still lives in the home, and he keeps communications limited to what the attorney approves.

Who will see the finished report?

The report goes only to the client named in the engagement and to any intended users identified at the start, such as a court, the other side in a divorce, or the IRS. Appraisers are bound by confidentiality rules, so Brian does not share the value or the report with neighbors, agents or anyone else without permission. Listing the intended users at the outset avoids any confusion later.

How are ADUs and converted garages handled?

Brian checks city or county permit records for every accessory unit, garage conversion and room addition. A permitted ADU is valued with comparable sales that include similar units, and its rental potential is considered. Space built without permits is described honestly and usually receives less weight than finished, permitted living area. El Cajon has many of these conversions, so the report explains exactly how each one was treated.

Does Brian appraise mobile and manufactured homes?

Manufactured homes on owned land, which are common in parts of East County and the backcountry, can be appraised as real property when they are permanently affixed. A coach on a rented space inside a park is generally classed as personal property instead of real estate, and that shifts the assignment a great deal. Describe the home, the land arrangement and whether the space is owned or rented in the request, and Brian will say whether the assignment fits.

Is rush service available?

Often it is. When a court date, tax deadline or closing on a family sale is close, Brian can sometimes move an assignment ahead of others already in progress. The written quote will show whether a rush fee applies and the delivery date he can commit to. Short timelines work best when the property address, effective date and access details arrive with the first request.

How is the home measured?

On a visit, Brian measures the exterior with a laser and tape and draws a floor plan following the ANSI standard for single-family living area. Garages, unpermitted rooms and finished space below grade are listed separately from gross living area. For a desktop report, square footage comes from county records, prior plans or listing data, and any conflict between sources is noted in the report.

When and how is payment made?

No deposit is required for typical assignments. Brian bills at delivery of the finished report and takes cards, checks and bank transfers. For attorneys, trustees and conservators, the bill can name the firm or the decedent's estate as payer, which is helpful when the fee needs to be paid from a trust account or reimbursed as an estate expense.

Is Brian licensed in California?

Brian's certification as a residential appraiser dates to 2004. The license is issued by the state Bureau of Real Estate Appraisers and can be verified on that agency's public lookup. It covers residential properties of one to four units without a value limit. He completes required continuing education, including the USPAP update course, in every renewal cycle.

Can Brian do an appraisal for a bank or financing?

No. Brian does not accept assignments ordered for financing, purchase credit or any bank or credit union decision. He has chosen to focus entirely on private clients, where the person who orders the report is the person who relies on it. His reports serve estates, trusts, divorce, property tax, insurance, bail bonds, immigration support and pre-purchase or pre-listing decisions made by owners themselves.

Can Brian review or challenge another appraisal?

Yes. If a spouse, co-heir or opposing party has produced a value that seems off, Brian can prepare an independent appraisal with his own comparable sales, or review the existing report for errors in data, method or reasoning. In litigation, he can explain the differences in a deposition or at trial. A second opinion is most useful when it starts from the same effective date and property description.
