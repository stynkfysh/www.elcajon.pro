About La Mesa

La Mesa sits on the rolling mesas and knolls between the City of San Diego and El Cajon, with Interstate 8 cutting east to west through its middle and State Route 125 skirting its eastern edge. Incorporated in 1912, the city grew around a rail stop that became La Mesa Village, where La Mesa Boulevard still carries storefronts from the early twentieth century. North of the freeway, Lake Murray and the slopes of Cowles Mountain frame the landscape, while Mount Helix rises just to the east. The terrain is rarely flat for long, and that topography shapes nearly every block.

Housing you find here

Near the village, the streets south of La Mesa Boulevard are lined with Craftsman bungalows and Spanish Revival cottages built between the 1910s and 1930s. Post-war expansion brought single-story ranch homes on quarter-acre lots across Northwest La Mesa toward Lake Murray, while Mount Nebo and the hills around Windsor Hills filled with split-levels and custom view homes in the 1950s and 1960s. Condominium and apartment buildings cluster along Fletcher Parkway, Grossmont Center Drive and Baltimore Drive, and the trolley stations at Grossmont and Spring Street have drawn newer multi-story housing.

Value levers in La Mesa

Views carry real weight in La Mesa. On Mount Nebo, a house with an ocean view or a view over to Mount Helix can bring far more than a comparable home one block lower with no view. Walking distance to the village and the trolley lines adds appeal, while frontage on Interstate 8, Fletcher Parkway or Jackson Drive tends to pull value down. Lot shape matters too, since steep hillside parcels can limit usable yard area and the room needed for an accessory dwelling unit.

Notes for an appraisal here

Many La Mesa homes are seventy to a hundred years old, so the appraiser has to separate original square footage from later additions and confirm what the county assessor actually records. Converted garages and enclosed porches turn up often in the older neighborhoods. For date-of-death and trust work, a retrospective effective date requires comparables from the months surrounding that date, not current sales. Brian also considers whether the view has protection or might be obstructed, and how much of a sloping lot can actually be used.

My parents' La Mesa bungalow had a back room added decades ago. Does that count toward the value?

It depends on what can be documented. Brian checks the county assessor's square footage, the city's permit history and the quality of the addition itself. A well-built, permitted room is generally treated as living area. An unpermitted addition may still contribute, but usually less, and the report explains the reasoning so an executor, attorney or court can follow how that space was handled.
