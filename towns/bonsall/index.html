About Bonsall

Bonsall is an unincorporated community spread along the San Luis Rey River and State Route 76, between Oceanside's eastern edge and Interstate 15. The valley floor holds river bottomland and pastures, while the hills to the north and south hold homes, groves and horse properties. Equestrian life is woven into the community, with training centers, riding trails and stables. Portions of Bonsall and the training center close by burned in the 2017 Lilac Fire, an enduring reminder of fire risk in the area.

Housing you find here

Housing in Bonsall is mostly single-family homes on one to several acres, built from the 1960s through recent years. Many properties include barns, covered arenas, round pens, hay storage and irrigated pasture. There are a few planned subdivisions in the hills, with larger homes on view lots, and older ranch homes near Mission Road and Old River Road. Wells, septic and private roads appear frequently, especially on parcels farther from SR-76.

Value levers in Bonsall

Equestrian improvements, level usable land and access dominate value here. A parcel with good fencing, barns and a short paved driveway sells differently from steep hillside land with limited flat space. Floodplain mapping along the San Luis Rey River restricts building on some lower parcels. Fire exposure and insurance cost are significant. Proximity to SR-76 and I-15 helps, but traffic on SR-76 affects homes closest to the highway.

Notes for an appraisal here

The appraiser verifies floodplain status, well and septic records, and permits for barns or arenas. Usable acreage is measured separately from total parcel size. Comparable sales come from other horse properties, sometimes in Fallbrook or Valley Center when Bonsall sales are limited. Homes rebuilt after the Lilac Fire may have newer effective ages, which needs to be reflected in the comparison.

Is a Bonsall horse property appraised differently from a regular house?

Yes. Brian looks for other equestrian sales with similar usable acreage, barns, arenas and fencing, then adjusts for differences. Water source, road access and floodplain limits are checked because they affect how buyers view the land. When few local horse properties have sold, he draws from comparable valleys and explains the selection in the report so the estate or court can follow it.
