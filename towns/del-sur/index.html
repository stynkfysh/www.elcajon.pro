About Del Sur

Del Sur is one of the newest residential areas in the City of San Diego, sitting inside the Black Mountain Ranch planning area north of SR-56 and west of 4S Ranch. Construction started in the mid-2000s and continued in phases into the 2020s. The land rolls over mesas and finger canyons that drain to the San Dieguito River, and many roads follow ridgelines with long views west to the ocean or east to the mountains. Brian's own office on Paseo Del Sur sits in this community.

Housing you find here

Housing in Del Sur is entirely modern, with builders using Craftsman, Spanish, farmhouse and Tuscan facades on two-story plans. Detached homes vary from compact courtyard layouts to large estate lots near the edges of the plan. Paseo Del Sur and Camino San Bernardo connect the residential villages to a town center with shops. Some neighborhoods include casitas or next-generation suites, and several of the most recent phases were built with solar panels as standard equipment under California energy code.

Value levers in Del Sur

Views, lot size and builder all drive value in Del Sur. Ridge-top homes facing west sell well above inward-facing lots, and estate sections command more than the smaller courtyard product. Special taxes through community facilities districts and the master HOA add to monthly cost. As the plan ages, original builder finishes versus later remodels start to matter. Some edges face wildfire-prone open space, which affects insurance.

Notes for an appraisal here

Builder sales and resales can both appear in the record, and the appraiser must decide whether a new-construction price with incentives reflects the resale market on the effective date. Special taxes, solar ownership and any casita all need confirmation. Many owners added pools or expanded patios soon after closing, so permit status is worth checking. For trust administration, consistent comparables from the same builder and village usually make the analysis strong.

My trust holds a Del Sur home bought new from the builder. Is the original purchase price a good guide?

Usually not on its own. Builder prices often included incentives or upgrades that resale buyers do not pay for in the same way, and the market may have moved since. Brian looks at resales of the same builder and plan close to the date being valued, then adjusts for lot, view and later improvements such as pools or landscaping.
