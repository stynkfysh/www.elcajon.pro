About Mission Hills

Mission Hills spreads across the high ground west of Hillcrest, ending at steep bluffs above Old Town, Mission Valley and the airport. Presidio Park, site of the original 1769 Spanish settlement, forms its northwestern edge. Horticulturist Kate Sessions, who planted much of Balboa Park, ran a nursery here, and the large mature trees lining many streets carry on her influence. Narrow, winding streets follow the canyon contours, and the small business district along Washington Street and Goldfinch serves the area.

Housing you find here

Most homes were built from about 1908 through the late 1930s. Craftsman, Prairie, Mission Revival, Spanish Colonial Revival and Tudor designs predominate, several by architects such as Irving Gill, Richard Requa and William Templeton Johnson. Lots vary widely, from small flat parcels to steep canyon-side sites with multilevel homes. The Fort Stockton Line and other historic districts protect large blocks of houses. Postwar homes and a few modern infill houses fill gaps, and duplexes sit near the commercial streets.

Value levers in Mission Hills

Bay, airport and Point Loma views command strong premiums on western and northern rim streets, while interior blocks trade on architecture and lot quality. Historic district status limits exterior changes but signals stability to buyers. Mills Act contracts reduce property taxes for designated homes, which buyers factor into price. Canyon lots can carry steep driveways, slope stability concerns and brush management obligations. Airport operations below the hill add some noise to the lowest western streets.

Notes for an appraisal here

Brian confirms whether a home is individually designated or inside a historic district, and whether a Mills Act contract runs with the land, since it transfers to heirs and buyers. Architect attribution, original materials and the quality of past restorations affect value. Canyon properties call for attention to retaining walls, slope and unpermitted lower-level rooms. With few sales each year, comparables may need to reach into earlier periods with market-condition adjustments.

A late father's Mission Hills house has a Mills Act contract. Does that change the appraisal?

It can. A Mills Act contract lowers the property tax burden for as long as it stays in force, and it binds the next owner to maintain the home's historic character. Brian notes the contract, its terms and its effect on buyer behavior, and compares the home with other designated properties where possible. For estate and Prop 19 purposes, the report identifies the contract clearly so the tax treatment is understood.
