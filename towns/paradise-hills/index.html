About Paradise Hills

Paradise Hills occupies rolling hills in the far southeastern part of San Diego, next to National City and north of the Sweetwater River valley. Its older single-family neighborhoods were largely built before World War II and through the 1950s and early 1960s. To the east, North and South Bay Terraces developed after 1969 with newer tracts and attached housing. Paradise Valley Road and Woodman Street are the main routes, and Paradise Canyon is kept as designated open space.

Housing you find here

Ranch homes from the 1950s and early 1960s dominate the western hills, typically single-story stucco or wood-sided houses with attached garages on sloping lots. Many sit on streets with views west toward the bay and the Coronado Bridge. North and South Bay Terraces hold 1970s and 1980s tract homes, townhomes and condominium complexes, several with HOAs and common pools. Infill houses and ADUs have appeared on larger lots in recent years. Lots near Alta View Drive tend to be terraced.

Value levers in Paradise Hills

Bay and city views lift prices noticeably on the higher streets. Lot usability matters on these slopes, since a flat backyard is less common than in valley tracts. Access to I-805 and shopping in National City supports demand. Bay Terraces attached homes trade on HOA dues, complex condition and parking. Homes along Paradise Valley Road and other busy streets draw less than those on quiet cul-de-sacs.

Notes for an appraisal here

Brian checks permits for additions, enclosed patios and garage conversions, which are frequent in the older tracts. HOA documents are reviewed for Bay Terraces condos and townhomes, including reserves and any special assessments. Slope, retaining walls and drainage are noted on hillside lots. Comparables are drawn from the same part of the community, because the older Paradise Hills tracts and the Bay Terraces developments attract different buyers.

How does an appraiser compare a Paradise Hills house with a Bay Terraces townhome?

They are different products, so they are not compared directly. Brian matches single-family homes with single-family sales from the older tracts and attached homes with townhome and condo sales from Bay Terraces or similar complexes. Within each group, he adjusts for view, lot or unit size, condition and HOA dues. That keeps the value grounded in what the actual buyer pool for that property type pays.
