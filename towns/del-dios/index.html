About Del Dios

Del Dios is a small unincorporated settlement on the north shore of Lake Hodges, strung along Del Dios Highway between Escondido and Rancho Santa Fe. The original town site lay lower in the valley and was flooded when Hodges Dam was finished in 1918, so the present community climbed the steep granite slopes above the water. Boulders, chaparral and narrow winding lanes define it. Hiking and biking trails of the San Dieguito River Park link it to the reservoir and the gorge below the dam.

Housing you find here

Homes are mostly individual builds rather than tracts. Some began as 1920s through 1950s weekend cabins and were expanded over the decades, while others are newer custom houses wedged onto steep lots with lake views. Lot shapes are irregular and street frontage is often limited. Septic is the norm, and several properties sit on shared or private roads. Parcel sizes vary sharply from small cabin lots near the highway to multi-acre hillside holdings.

Value levers in Del Dios

Lake views, topography and access dominate value. A home with a clear outlook over Lake Hodges and level parking trades very differently from one reached by a steep, shared driveway. Wildfire exposure is high in the surrounding chaparral, and buyers weigh insurance carefully. Traffic noise along Del Dios Highway affects homes closest to the road. Proximity to Rancho Santa Fe and Escondido helps demand, but limited inventory keeps sales few.

Notes for an appraisal here

Many Del Dios homes have grown through additions that may never have been permitted, so county records must be compared against what exists. Septic capacity and condition matter because steep sites limit replacement options. With so few local sales, the appraiser may draw from rural hillside properties in Escondido, Harmony Grove or the San Pasqual area and adjust carefully, explaining each choice in the report.

Grandma's Del Dios cabin was added onto several times. Will unpermitted rooms hurt the trust valuation?

Brian compares the county permit history with the house as it stands and describes any space that lacks permits. Unpermitted rooms are typically given less weight than permitted living area, and the report explains why. If the additions are well built and typical for the area, he notes how buyers in Del Dios generally treat them, giving the trustee a clear basis for the value conclusion.
