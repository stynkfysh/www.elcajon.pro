About Clairemont

Clairemont spreads over a wide mesa bounded by Interstate 5, Interstate 805, State Route 52 and Mission Bay, and finger canyons cut through it, draining toward Rose Canyon and Tecolote Canyon. It was promoted from 1950 onward by developers Carlos Tavares and Lou Burgener as a village within a city, with the name drawn from Claire Tavares. Balboa Avenue, Genesee Avenue and Clairemont Mesa Boulevard carry traffic and shopping, including Clairemont Town Square, which opened in the 1950s and was later rebuilt.

Housing you find here

The housing is defined by one-story ranch homes from the 1950s and 1960s, many put up by a handful of tract builders using similar layouts of about 1,000 to 1,600 square feet. Lots commonly run 5,000 to 7,500 square feet, with larger, irregular parcels on canyon rims. North Clairemont and Clairemont Mesa East have streets ending at open space, while Clairemont Mesa West rises toward bay views. Clairemont has become one of the city's busiest areas for accessory dwelling unit permits on deep lots.

Value levers in Clairemont

Lot size, canyon frontage, view and degree of remodeling drive most differences. Because original floor plans repeat, buyers compare homes closely, so updated kitchens, added bedrooms and solar make a measurable difference. Proximity to the trolley, freeways and job centers supports demand. Busy arterials and the flight path of MCAS Miramar to the north on some eastern streets can weigh on value, and canyon brush brings fire considerations.

Notes for an appraisal here

Repeated tract models make Clairemont well suited to direct comparison, but additions and ADUs require permit verification since many were built informally decades ago. The appraiser should note whether an ADU is separately metered and rentable. Canyon lots may carry brush management zones and slope issues. Sales should come from the same sub-area when possible, since view and canyon lots in Clairemont Mesa West trade apart from interior blocks.

Does a backyard ADU in Clairemont add full value in a divorce appraisal?

It depends on permits and the local market for similar properties. Brian confirms the unit was finaled by the City, then compares sales of Clairemont homes with and without permitted ADUs. Buyers often pay a premium for rentable space, but rarely the full construction cost. An unpermitted unit receives far less weight, and the report explains how each was treated.
