About Bay Park

Bay Park climbs the western edge of the Clairemont mesa, rising from Morena Boulevard and Interstate 5 to ridge streets with long views over Mission Bay and the ocean beyond. Tecolote Canyon borders it on the east and south, giving many streets canyon edges and open space. The Mid-Coast Trolley extension added the Clairemont Drive and Tecolote Road stations along Morena in 2021. The Morena corridor below holds restaurants, design shops and older light industrial buildings that are slowly turning into housing.

Housing you find here

A large part of Bay Park was developed during the late 1940s and the 1950s with modest one-story houses, frequently stucco ranch homes on lots of roughly 5,000 to 7,000 square feet. Streets climbing from Morena, including Milton Street and the blocks around Burgener Boulevard, hold the strongest view lots. Many original homes have gained second stories or been replaced with contemporary designs oriented toward the bay. Small apartment buildings sit closer to Morena, and accessory units have multiplied on larger parcels.

Value levers in Bay Park

View quality explains most of the spread between Bay Park sales. A clear Mission Bay and ocean panorama can add substantial value over a nearly identical house with no view. Freeway noise from Interstate 5 affects lower streets, and slopes can raise construction costs for additions. Trolley access and the Morena Corridor Specific Plan have brought new density nearby, which can affect views and traffic for some owners while drawing interest from buyers who want transit.

Notes for an appraisal here

Second-story additions and view-oriented remodels should be confirmed against permits, because unpermitted upper floors are a known risk in older tracts. The appraiser needs to describe whether views are protected or exposed to future building below. Canyon-edge lots may have usable area limits, slope stability questions or brush management requirements. Comparables should match view tier, and older effective dates may require tracking sales before and after the 2021 trolley opening.

Will a Bay Park view be valued the same if newer buildings could block it later?

Not always. Brian reviews zoning on the parcels below a subject property, notes whether height rules or slope protect the outlook, and compares sales with secure views against those where buyers saw future building risk. The appraisal states the view as it exists on the effective date while explaining any visible threat that buyers would likely weigh.
