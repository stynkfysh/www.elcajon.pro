About Jacumba Hot Springs

Jacumba Hot Springs sits along Old Highway 80 at about 2,800 feet, right against the international border in far southeastern San Diego County. Natural hot springs drew visitors in the 1920s, when the town was a popular resort stop on the highway to Imperial Valley. The San Diego and Arizona Railway passes through on its way to the Carrizo Gorge and the Goat Canyon Trestle. To the northeast, the Desert View Tower in In-Ko-Pah Gorge overlooks the desert below.

Housing you find here

The old town grid holds small lots with cottages, older frame houses and some adobe or stone buildings dating from the 1920s through the 1950s. Manufactured homes fill many lots, and larger parcels surround the town on desert slopes. A handful of newer homes and remodeled cottages appear among older structures. Vacant lots are plentiful. Boulder hills and desert scrub frame the community, and the hot springs spa remains a local focal point.

Value levers in Jacumba Hot Springs

Very low land prices and the spa appeal to retirees and investors, while distance to services, summer heat and border proximity limit demand. Large solar projects nearby have changed views and local attitudes. Properties with renovated interiors and reliable utilities sell more easily. The town's small size means a single sale can shift perceptions of value noticeably. Many lots remain undeveloped, which affects absorption.

Notes for an appraisal here

Sales activity is extremely limited, and comparables may need to come from Boulevard or even Ocotillo across the county line, with care. Many older buildings have unpermitted alterations. Water and sewer service vary, as Jacumba has a community services district but outlying parcels may rely on wells and septic. Manufactured homes require proper classification. Older effective dates are common for estate work here, and historical data is sparse.

Why are Jacumba property values so different from the rest of San Diego County?

Jacumba Hot Springs is remote, small and close to the border, and it has many vacant lots, so its market operates on its own terms. Brian relies on local sales and, when needed, comparable desert towns rather than countywide averages.
