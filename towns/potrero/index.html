About Potrero

Potrero is Spanish for pasture, and the valley looks the part even now. It sits along State Route 94 at about 2,300 feet, a short drive north of the Tecate border crossing. Grassy flats and oaks fill the valley floor, with Potrero Peak and granite ridges around it. Potrero County Park offers camping and shade among oaks. The 2007 Harris Fire, which began near Harris Ranch Road, burned much of the surrounding area.

Housing you find here

The typical Potrero home stands on one to ten or more acres. Ranch houses from the 1960s to 1990s are common, alongside manufactured homes and older farmhouses near the highway. Several properties include barns, horse pens and agricultural outbuildings. Some residences were rebuilt after the Harris Fire, so construction dates vary widely within short distances. Roads such as Potrero Valley Road and Harris Ranch Road lead to the scattered homes away from Highway 94.

Value levers in Potrero

Potrero appeals to buyers wanting land, privacy and horse property at backcountry prices. Distance to San Diego, a two-lane highway commute and proximity to the border all limit demand. Fire exposure is high and affects insurance. Usable level acreage with good well production and room for animals brings stronger interest, while steep or brushy parcels move slowly. Commercial frontage along SR-94 forms a small separate segment.

Notes for an appraisal here

Rebuilt homes after the 2007 fire may be newer than their neighbors, so effective age needs care. Permit records for barns, guest quarters and enclosed porches should be reviewed. Nearly all properties depend on private wells and septic systems. Comparable sales are limited and often come from Campo, Dulzura or Jamul. Manufactured homes need proper classification. Easements and access along dirt roads should be confirmed in title records.

My parents rebuilt their Potrero home after the Harris Fire. Will the appraiser see it as newer?

Yes. Brian records the actual year built and the quality of the rebuilt home, then compares it with sales of similar-aged construction when possible. A post-fire rebuild often has updated wiring, roofing and insulation, which buyers notice. The report explains how effective age and condition were set relative to older properties nearby.
