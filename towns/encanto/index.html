About Encanto

Encanto covers the hills of southeastern San Diego east of Interstate 805 and west of Lemon Grove. Annexed to the city in 1916, it began as a semi-rural district of large lots, small farms and orchards on the slopes above Chollas Creek. Branches of the creek still cut canyons through it, and Imperial Avenue and Market Street cross it east to west. The Encanto/62nd Street trolley station and the Market Creek development near Euclid Avenue serve the western portion.

Housing you find here

Homes span from the 1910s through recent infill. Many older houses sit on large, irregular lots, some with a history of horses, chickens or fruit trees. Much of the area, notably Emerald Hills and Valencia Park, is made up of 1950s and 1960s postwar tracts with ranch houses on terraced lots. Lots vary widely, and many have been split or had second units added. A few streets remain unpaved or lack sidewalks, a leftover of the rural era.

Value levers in Encanto

Larger lots, canyon views and room for ADUs or lot splits support values. Access to the trolley, SR-94 and I-805 helps. Homes on busy arterials or beside Chollas Creek may face noise or flood mapping issues. Unimproved streets and older infrastructure can weigh on appeal in some pockets. Buyers often look here for space they cannot find closer to downtown, so usable flat land carries real weight.

Notes for an appraisal here

Brian checks lot size, topography, subdivision potential and permit history. Unpermitted additions, converted garages and older second units are common. Flood mapping is reviewed for creek-adjacent lots, and canyon parcels get a wildfire and brush management review. Comparables need to reflect lot size and usability, since a large flat parcel and a large steep one sell very differently. Unpaved frontage and easements for access are noted when they affect value.

A family's Encanto lot is much larger than most on the street. Does that add a lot of value?

Possibly. A larger lot can support an ADU, a lot split or simply more usable yard, and buyers often pay for that. Brian considers zoning, access, slope and utilities, then compares the property with other large-lot sales in Encanto and nearby areas. If much of the extra land is steep canyon or cannot be built on, its contribution is smaller, and the report explains the difference.
