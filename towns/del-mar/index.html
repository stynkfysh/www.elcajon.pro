About Del Mar

Del Mar is a coastal city of roughly two square miles, incorporated in 1959, set between Solana Beach and the Torrey Pines area of San Diego. The San Dieguito River and the Del Mar Fairgrounds, home to the summer horse racing season, mark its north end. Camino del Mar is the main street through the village, and the railroad follows the bluffs above the beach south of town. The terrain rises sharply from the sand onto hillside neighborhoods with long ocean views.

Housing you find here

The beach colony along the sand north of the village holds small lots packed with beach houses, many rebuilt as two-story contemporaries. Hillside streets east of Camino del Mar, up toward Crest Canyon and the Del Mar Hills, hold 1950s through 1970s custom homes, often redesigned to open up views. Olde Del Mar, south of the village, has larger lots on winding streets near the bluffs. Condominiums are few, and most properties are detached homes on parcels shaped by the steep terrain.

Value levers in Del Mar

Ocean views and beach proximity push prices to the top of the county market. Lot size, rebuild potential and design review define what can be built. Fairgrounds events and race-season traffic affect nearby homes, and the rail line's bluff-top route brings train noise and periodic bluff stabilization work. Low-lying parcels near the river mouth carry flood risk. The village's limited inventory supports values, but also means few sales in any given month.

Notes for an appraisal here

Del Mar's strict design review and Coastal Commission oversight influence highest and best use and the feasibility of additions. Flood-zone status near the San Dieguito River and in the beach colony should be checked. Sales are thin, so the appraiser often needs a longer time window and must adjust for market change. View corridors, lot size and condition dominate adjustments, and land value can outweigh the existing improvements on many parcels.

With so few sales in Del Mar, how does an appraiser support value for a date of death?

Brian extends the search window and, when necessary, uses nearby Solana Beach, Carmel Valley or La Jolla sales, adjusting for location and time. A market-conditions analysis brings older sales forward to the effective date. The report spells out the reason behind each comparable so the court, heirs and taxing agencies can trace the logic.
