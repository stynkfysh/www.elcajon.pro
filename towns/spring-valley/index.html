About Spring Valley

Spring Valley is a sizable unincorporated area southeast of La Mesa and Lemon Grove, covering valleys and rounded hills whose runoff flows to the Sweetwater River. Historian Hubert Howe Bancroft owned a ranch here in the late 1800s, and his adobe ranch house is preserved as the Bancroft Ranch House Museum, a National Historic Landmark. State Route 94 and State Route 125 cross the community, with Jamacha Boulevard and Sweetwater Springs Boulevard serving as main local routes. Dictionary Hill, an open-space ridge, rises in the middle of the community.

Housing you find here

Spring Valley grew mainly after World War II, and modest ranch homes from the 1950s and 1960s fill the lower streets around Jamacha Boulevard and Kempton Street. Hillside areas, including the slopes around Dictionary Hill, carry larger lots, some with irregular shapes and long private driveways. Along Sweetwater Springs Boulevard, 1970s and 1980s tracts and townhome communities with associations appear. Older homes on half-acre parcels, sometimes with fruit trees and space for animals, remain scattered throughout as a reminder of the area's agricultural past.

Value levers in Spring Valley

Spring Valley prices often move with view and lot utility. A house perched on a knoll with a city or mountain outlook commands more than a similar home on a valley street. Corner lots on busy roads like Jamacha Boulevard or Sweetwater Road tend to sell for less. Larger flat lots that can support an accessory dwelling unit attract attention, and county zoning differs from neighboring city rules. Freeway access is convenient but brings noise to homes along SR-94 and SR-125.

Notes for an appraisal here

Spring Valley addresses cover several census places, including La Presa and Casa de Oro, so Brian confirms the parcel's location before selecting comparables. Unincorporated status means building permits come from San Diego County, not a city, and older additions often predate permit records or never had them. Long driveways, shared easements and irregular hillside parcels also need confirmation from the parcel map. For retrospective valuations in estates and trusts, he makes sure the comparables closed near the date of death.

A Spring Valley mailing address covers a wide area. How does Brian choose comparables?

Spring Valley addresses stretch across several distinct areas, from Dictionary Hill to the reservoir side of La Presa. Brian maps the subject parcel and searches for sales with similar terrain, lot size, age and outlook close by, rather than relying on the city name in the address. That keeps a valley-floor ranch home from being compared with a hillside view property simply because both share the same mailing city.
