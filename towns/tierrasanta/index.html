About Tierrasanta

Tierrasanta occupies a mesa ringed by canyons and open land, with Interstate 15 to the west, State Route 52 and Marine Corps Air Station Miramar to the north, Mission Trails Regional Park to the east and the San Diego River gorge below it on the south. The land was once part of Camp Elliott, a World War II training area, and unexploded ordnance cleanup in surrounding open space has continued for decades. Tierrasanta Boulevard and Santo Road are the main routes.

Housing you find here

Building started in the early 1970s and ran through the 1980s, yielding detached houses, townhomes and condos laid out along greenbelts with a recreation center at the middle. Many tracts carry homeowner associations, some with pools and common landscaping. Single-family homes are typically two-story with stucco or wood siding on moderate lots, and many back onto canyon open space. Attached homes are grouped in clusters along greenbelt paths, and newer infill has been rare because surrounding open space is protected.

Value levers in Tierrasanta

Canyon backing, outlooks across open space and association amenities explain most of the price spread. Direct trail access into Mission Trails is a strong draw, and the community's isolation from through traffic appeals to many buyers. Jet and helicopter activity from MCAS Miramar is noticeable on northern streets. For townhomes and condominiums, dues and reserve health influence price. Canyon-edge homes face wildfire exposure, and 1970s plumbing, windows and roofs often need replacement.

Notes for an appraisal here

Association documents, including dues, reserve studies and special assessments, are needed for nearly every Tierrasanta sale, even for some detached homes. Canyon-edge parcels should be checked against fire severity maps and brush clearance rules. Patio covers, room additions and pools built over the decades need permit verification. Because tracts differ in builder, amenities and age, comparables should come from the same tract or association when the data allows.

Does the old military history of Tierrasanta affect home values today?

The former Camp Elliott history is well known locally, and the ordnance cleanup has focused on surrounding open space rather than developed lots. Brian reviews whether any recent disclosures or reports affected buyer behavior and compares sales across the community. If no measurable impact appears in sales, the appraisal says so and explains why.
