About Palomar Mountain

Palomar Mountain rises above 5,000 feet in northern San Diego County, reached by South Grade Road (County Route S6) from Pauma Valley and East Grade Road (S7) from Lake Henshaw. Incense cedar, white fir, oak and pine cover the upper slopes. Palomar Mountain State Park holds Doane Pond and the Boucher Hill lookout, and Caltech's Palomar Observatory occupies the summit area. A general store and restaurant sit at the junction near the top.

Housing you find here

Homes on the mountain are mostly cabins and small houses on wooded lots along Crestline Road, Birch Hill Road and other narrow streets. Construction dates span the 1920s to the present, and many structures have been enlarged over time. Parcels vary from small cabin lots up to several acres. Steep driveways, wood stoves, wraparound decks and metal roofs are typical, and some homes serve as vacation rentals or seasonal residences.

Value levers in Palomar Mountain

Forest settings and cool summers draw buyers, but steep winding roads, snow and distance from town limit the pool. Fire exposure is very high, and insurance availability affects marketability. Water comes from small water systems or private wells, and supply reliability matters. Properties with views toward the coast or the desert, level parking and year-round access tend to sell first and for more.

Notes for an appraisal here

The appraiser should confirm water source, septic condition and any shared well or water company membership. Many cabins have additions with unclear permit history. Sales are few and seasonal, so data may need to span several years. Road access, especially on private or unpaved sections, should be documented. Short-term rental history may be relevant but must be separated from real property value.

Is a Palomar Mountain cabin with shared well water harder to appraise?

It can require extra documentation. Brian reviews any water company or shared well agreements, membership rights and known supply problems. Buyers on the mountain are often cautious about water, so the report notes how the source compares with that of comparable sales and whether an adjustment is supported by the local evidence on the mountain.
