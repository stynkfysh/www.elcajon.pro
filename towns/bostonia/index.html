About Bostonia

Bostonia covers the northeastern corner of the El Cajon valley, part inside El Cajon city limits and part in an unincorporated census place north of Broadway and east of State Route 67. The district traces back to the 1880s, when a general store opened at Second Street and Broadway and packing houses served surrounding orchards and vineyards. Residents of the southern portion voted to join El Cajon in 1953, leaving the northern section under county jurisdiction. The land is mostly flat valley floor that lifts gently toward the Winter Gardens hills.

Housing you find here

Bostonia's housing reflects its agricultural roots: older single-story homes on deep lots, some built in the 1940s and 1950s on former orchard parcels, sit beside 1960s and 1970s tract houses. Larger parcels with detached garages, workshops and room for an extra structure remain common on streets near Bostonia Street and Pepper Drive. Mobile home parks and apartment complexes cluster along Broadway and North Second Street. Infill subdivisions and small-lot projects have appeared on former large parcels over the past few decades.

Value levers in Bostonia

Lot depth and usable land drive much of the difference in Bostonia, especially where a parcel can support a detached accessory unit or has space for a workshop or recreational vehicle parking. Proximity to Broadway's commercial traffic tends to depress value, while quieter interior streets fare better. Gillespie Field lies to the west, and properties near its flight paths can see some effect. Older homes with original systems often sell well below remodeled ones, so condition shapes pricing more than square footage does.

Notes for an appraisal here

Bostonia straddles two jurisdictions, so Brian confirms whether a parcel is inside El Cajon or in the unincorporated county area before checking permits and zoning. That distinction affects which agency holds building records and what accessory unit rules apply. Older parcels may carry unrecorded additions, converted garages and accessory structures. Comparables from the city side and the county pocket can be used together if location and lot characteristics align, but the report should explain how they were selected.

Is my Bostonia house in El Cajon or the county, and why does that matter for an appraisal?

Both are possible, since Bostonia is split between the city and an unincorporated pocket. Brian checks the parcel's jurisdiction first, because it determines where permit records live and which accessory dwelling rules apply. Market value itself is driven by location and property features, so comparables can come from either side, but the report notes the jurisdiction and any permit findings tied to it.
