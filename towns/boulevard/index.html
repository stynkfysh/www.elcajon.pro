Boulevard lies past the edge of Brian's inspection map. Each Boulevard order is handled with one of the three no-visit formats, and no curbside or walk-through option exists there.

About Boulevard

Boulevard lies near the eastern edge of San Diego County's mountain region, at roughly 3,400 feet along Old Highway 80 and just off Interstate 8. Granite outcrops, chaparral and scattered oaks give way to desert scrub to the east. Wind turbines on nearby ridges reflect the area's role in renewable energy development, and McCain Valley Road heads north into Bureau of Land Management land. Small settlements such as Live Oak Springs, Manzanita and Bankhead Springs share the area.

Housing you find here

Housing consists mainly of manufactured homes and modest stick-built houses on parcels from one to forty acres. Some older cabins and roadside buildings date to the Old Highway 80 era of the 1920s through the 1950s. Live Oak Springs has small lots and older structures around its historic resort grounds. Owner-built homes, workshops and container storage are common, and many properties rely on solar power and propane in addition to grid service where available.

Value levers in Boulevard

Low land prices, open space and long views draw some buyers, while distance, wind and limited services hold values down. Wind and solar projects have been a recurring local controversy; turbine views and transmission lines can affect appeal for nearby homes. Fire exposure is high in chaparral areas. Properties with good wells, reliable access and modest views of the desert or Tecate Peak are the strongest sellers.

Notes for an appraisal here

Market data is thin, so the appraiser should expect to use sales from Campo, Jacumba Hot Springs and Pine Valley. Proximity to turbines or transmission lines may justify an adjustment if the market supports one. Manufactured home classification, foundation type and permit status need confirmation. Wells, septic, propane and off-grid systems must be documented, and some parcels have access through BLM or tribal land that needs title review.

Do the wind turbines near Boulevard lower property values for an appraisal?

Brian lets the local sales answer that. He compares homes with clear turbine or transmission line views against similar ones without them, when such pairs exist. If buyers have consistently paid less, the report reflects that through an adjustment. When the data is too limited, he explains the uncertainty rather than applying a number that cannot be supported.
