About City of San Diego

The City of San Diego covers more than 370 square miles, stretching from the Tijuana River Valley and San Ysidro at the border north to Rancho Bernardo and Black Mountain Ranch. Its terrain is a series of flat-topped mesas cut by deep canyons, with Mission Bay, the San Diego River and Mission Valley splitting the middle. Interstates 5, 8, 15 and 805 tie it together. For an appraiser, the city is less one market than dozens of distinct submarkets sharing a single set of zoning, permit and historic-preservation rules.

Housing you find here

Housing in the city tracks its growth outward from the bay. Victorian and Craftsman houses survive in Golden Hill, Sherman Heights and North Park. Postwar tract homes fill Clairemont, Allied Gardens and Serra Mesa, many on canyon-edge lots. The 1970s and 1980s brought planned communities such as Mira Mesa, Tierrasanta, Rancho Penasquitos and Scripps Ranch, while Carmel Valley and Del Sur carry newer homes, master HOAs and Mello-Roos districts. Downtown and Uptown add thousands of condominiums, from converted apartments to glass towers.

Value levers in City of San Diego

Nearness to the coast moves value more than anything else, and west of Interstate 5 the 30-foot coastal height limit governs what can be built. Elsewhere, canyon views, lot usability and freeway or flight-path noise from San Diego International and MCAS Miramar separate otherwise similar homes. Historic designation, Mills Act contracts and the city's permissive ADU rules can add or limit value depending on the parcel. Community facilities districts in northern planned areas affect what buyers will pay.

Notes for an appraisal here

Because one ZIP can contain several unlike neighborhoods, comparable selection has to follow canyon lines and tract boundaries rather than postal codes. Brian checks permit history through the city's Development Services records, since enclosed patios, garage conversions and unpermitted ADUs are widespread. Brush management zones on canyon lots, coastal development rules and any Mills Act contract all bear on value. For retrospective dates, he pulls sales from the exact submarket as it stood on that date.

A parent left homes in two different parts of San Diego. Can one appraiser value both?

Yes. Brian has worked across the whole city for more than two decades, from coastal neighborhoods to the inland mesas. Each property gets its own analysis using sales from its own submarket, because a canyon-rim house in Clairemont and a condo in East Village answer to different buyers. Both reports can carry the same effective date, which keeps the trust or estate accounting consistent.
