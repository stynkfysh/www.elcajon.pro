About North Park

North Park lies directly east of Balboa Park, centered on the crossroads of University Avenue and 30th Street. Streetcar service in the 1910s and 1920s turned former lemon groves into one of San Diego's first large bungalow suburbs. The core now has breweries, restaurants and the restored Observatory North Park theater, and the residential streets extend north to the edge of Mission Valley and south to Switzer Canyon and Juniper Street. The 1924 North Park Water Tower still stands on Idaho Street.

Housing you find here

Craftsman bungalows, many from 1910 to 1930, make up most of the housing, with a scattering of Spanish Revival cottages and a few Prairie and Mission style homes. Designated districts such as Burlingame, the Dryden Historic District along 28th Street and Shirley Ann Place protect concentrated groups. Postwar stucco apartments from the 1960s and 1970s replaced many houses, and newer mid-rise buildings line University and El Cajon Boulevard. Detached backyard units and garage conversions are common behind original houses.

Value levers in North Park

Original Craftsman detailing, a usable yard and off-street parking support the highest prices. Blocks near Morley Field and the Balboa Park edge carry extra appeal, while lots along the busiest arterials draw less. Transit-oriented zoning lets owners add units, so lots with room for an ADU or redevelopment hold land value well. Flipped houses with heavy modernization compete differently from intact originals, and buyers sort clearly between the two.

Notes for an appraisal here

Unpermitted granny flats and converted garages turn up often, and Brian checks city permit records before counting any secondary unit as legal income or living area. Historic designation affects what an heir or buyer can change. Older homes may still have original knob-and-tube wiring, raised foundations or sewer laterals that need replacement, all of which affect condition ratings. Comparables should reflect the same era and degree of updating.

Two siblings inherited a North Park bungalow with a back cottage. Does the cottage add value?

It depends on whether the cottage was permitted. A legally built ADU adds both living area and rental potential that buyers pay for. A unit built without permits still adds some value, though buyers mark it down for the expense and risk of either legalizing it or tearing it out. Brian checks city records and explains in the report how the cottage was treated, which helps when heirs are dividing the property.
