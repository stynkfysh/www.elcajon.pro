About San Elijo Hills

San Elijo Hills occupies the high ground in the southern part of San Marcos, rising toward Double Peak at roughly 1,640 feet. Development began around 2000 under a single master plan, and the community now reaches from San Elijo Road up into steep canyons bordering the Elfin Forest open space. A town center with a grocery, plaza and shops sits near the heart of the plan. The terrain is all slopes and ridgelines, so most streets curve along graded pads that step down the hillsides.

Housing you find here

Nearly every home here dates from roughly 2001 forward, built by several production builders in separately branded neighborhoods. Product includes attached townhomes near the town center, mid-size detached homes on compact lots, and larger estate homes on ridge pads with canyon or ocean-facing outlooks. Floor plans of 2,500 to 4,000 square feet are common in the detached sections. Homeowners association membership is universal, and many neighborhoods sit under a sub-association as well as the master association.

Value levers in San Elijo Hills

View quality is the clearest separator. Homes on the upper ridges near Double Peak Park with westward sight lines toward the Pacific trade well above interior lots with backyard slopes facing another row of houses. Mello-Roos special taxes apply across most of the community and are a recurring factor buyers weigh. Wildfire exposure at the open space interface affects insurance availability. Pad size and usable yard matter because steep manufactured slopes often make up a large share of the parcel.

Notes for an appraisal here

Usable yard area deserves careful measurement since recorded lot size can be mostly slope. The appraiser confirms the remaining CFD obligation and both association dues. For divorce and trust work, builder upgrade packages such as extended rooms and covered loggias should be matched to plans in comparables. Ocean views need to be verified from inside rather than assumed from elevation alone, because a neighboring rooftop can block the line.

Does the Mello-Roos tax on a San Elijo Hills home lower its value in a divorce appraisal?

Brian accounts for the special tax by choosing comparable sales that carry similar assessments, which usually means other San Elijo Hills resales. Because buyers of those homes paid the same type of tax, its effect is already reflected in the sale prices. He documents the current annual amount and the payoff horizon so both parties and their attorneys see how it was handled.
