East El Cajon Hills (Greenfield and Granite Hills Drive) is inside the City of El Cajon, where Brian inspects in person, so inspected and desktop reports are both available.

The neighborhood

On the city's eastern edge, El Cajon climbs from the valley floor onto the lower slopes around Greenfield Drive, Granite Hills Drive and the eastern stretch of Madison Avenue. City territory and unincorporated Granite Hills interlock here, so neighbors can fall under different jurisdictions. Albert Van Zanten Park and John F. Kennedy Park provide open space, and the Greenfield Drive interchange connects to Interstate 8. Rounded granite boulders, a signature of the eastern valley, appear in yards and on undeveloped slopes, and higher streets look across the basin toward Fletcher Hills and Mount Helix.

Homes and lots

Single-family homes dominate, from 1950s ranches on the flatter lower streets to 1970s and 1980s split-levels and larger custom homes higher up. Lots grow larger as elevation rises, and some exceed a third of an acre with pools, RV pads or detached workshops. Granite outcrops are often incorporated into landscaping or limit the usable yard. Small planned developments with HOAs appear in places, while most homes sit on individual lots without association dues. Many homes have been expanded with rear rooms or upper levels, and solar panels are common on south-facing roofs. Lower streets near Madison resemble the valley tracts, with level yards and attached garages.

How the market treats it

Views, lot size and privacy drive the upper end here, while lower streets trade much like other valley neighborhoods. Proximity to Interstate 8 adds convenience but some noise for nearby homes. Large lots appeal to buyers seeking space for vehicles or an added unit. Toward the eastern rim, wildfire exposure and insurance cost are part of buyers' calculations, and steep driveways or heavy rock can reduce the utility of a site.

Appraisal checkpoints

The first step is confirming whether a property is in the city or in unincorporated Granite Hills, since that determines which permit records and zoning rules apply. Comparable sales should share similar elevation, lot size and view. Granite outcrops, slope and retaining walls can limit usable area and deserve description. Fire hazard mapping on the eastern slopes should be identified, and the appraiser checks whether market data shows any measurable effect.

Questions

How does the appraiser handle homes that border unincorporated Granite Hills?

Brian first confirms the jurisdiction from parcel data. He then looks for comparables with similar terrain and lot size from both the city and county sides, since buyers often see the area as one market. Differences in permits, services or zoning are noted in the report whenever they could reasonably affect value.

Do granite boulders on a lot affect its value?

They can work either way. Large rocks may add character and privacy, yet they can also reduce usable yard area, complicate pools or additions and increase site costs. Brian describes how the rocks affect the lot's utility, including access for equipment, and looks for comparables with similar site conditions on nearby slopes.
