Cajon Heights, El Cajon: home appraisals

Low hills just southwest of downtown, close to the trolley

Cajon Heights is inside the City of El Cajon, where Brian inspects in person, so inspected and desktop reports are both available.

The neighborhood

Cajon Heights straddles Interstate 8 southwest of downtown, on gently rising ground between West Main Street and the low hills around Renette Park. It sits within walking distance of the El Cajon Transit Center on South Marshall Avenue and only a short drive from Parkway Plaza. The neighborhood lacks the high ridgelines of Fletcher Hills, yet the land lifts enough that some streets gain partial views back across the valley floor. It is a practical, close-in residential area where older single-family blocks give way to apartment buildings near the arterials.

Homes and lots

Single-family homes here mostly date from the 1940s through the 1960s, with modest stucco ranches and some earlier cottages on lots of roughly a fifth of an acre or less. Streets such as La Sombra Drive and Blackthorne Avenue lean toward slightly sloped parcels, while blocks nearer Johnson Avenue are flat. Apartment complexes and duplexes appear along the busier corridors. Garage conversions and rear units are common, reflecting the area's long history of owner additions. Yards tend to be compact, and mature pepper trees, palms and citrus shade many of the older lots. Few properties here carry association dues.

How the market treats it

Easy freeway and trolley access appeals to commuters, but the same proximity brings traffic and noise for homes closest to Interstate 8 and Johnson Avenue. Interior streets with slight elevation typically outperform blocks backing to commercial uses. Condition varies a great deal, so updated homes and dated originals can sell far apart on the same street. Lots with room for an ADU have drawn added interest since state rules eased.

Appraisal checkpoints

Freeway adjacency should be matched in the comparables rather than adjusted from thin assumptions. Converted garages and back units require permit verification before they are treated as living area or as legal second dwellings. Where apartments border a single-family lot, external influences deserve a clear description. Sales from the same side of Interstate 8 generally provide better evidence than those from Fletcher Hills to the northwest, where lots and views differ.

Questions

How does freeway noise near Interstate 8 affect a Cajon Heights appraisal?

Houses backing onto or facing the freeway tend to sell below similar homes a few blocks off, though the gap varies. Sound walls, orientation and lot depth all play a part. Brian prefers comparables with similar exposure and only quantifies an adjustment when paired sales or market evidence support one.

Is a back unit in Cajon Heights counted as a second home?

Only if it was permitted as a dwelling. A legal ADU or guest unit can contribute meaningful value and sometimes rental income. An unpermitted structure is described honestly, and any value assigned to it reflects what the market would pay given the uncertainty, not what a permitted unit would bring.
