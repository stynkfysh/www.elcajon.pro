The neighborhood

North of downtown and east of the Gillespie Field district, the city's north-central neighborhoods stretch along Broadway, North Mollison Avenue and the residential streets around Bostonia Park. The area borders unincorporated Bostonia, and many locals use that name for blocks that are actually inside El Cajon. The land is the flat to gently rising northern valley floor, with views of the Lakeside hills and El Cajon Mountain on clear days. Broadway carries a commercial strip of shops and services, while side streets hold quieter residential pockets lined with mature trees.

Homes and lots

A large portion of the homes are postwar single-family houses, mostly from the 1950s to the 1970s, on lots ranging from 6,000 square feet to a quarter acre. Streets such as Clarendon, Burnet and Manor Drive show the typical one-story stucco ranch, often with converted garages or added rooms. Apartments and condominiums cluster near Broadway and Mollison. Several manufactured home communities lie in or near the area, and some older parcels still have large backyards from earlier rural use. Detached workshops, covered patios and block-wall perimeter fencing are frequent additions on these older lots.

How the market treats it

Buyers here look for value relative to the rest of the city, so condition and lot features strongly influence price. Interior blocks away from Broadway traffic typically sell better. Lots with room for RV parking or an ADU draw added interest. The unincorporated Bostonia boundary creates a patchwork, but buyers tend to see the area as continuous, which means jurisdiction matters more to permits than to price. Updated homes on quiet streets attract the strongest offers.

Appraisal checkpoints

Permit records for this area sit with two agencies, so the first task is establishing whether a parcel belongs to the city or to county-governed Bostonia. Converted garages and additions are common and need verification. Comparables from both sides of the boundary may be valid when housing and lots are alike. Manufactured home properties require separate analysis of title, foundation and land tenure, and commercial frontage along Broadway should be noted as an external influence.

Questions

Is a home near Bostonia Park in El Cajon or in Bostonia?

It may be either. Bostonia Park itself is a city park, yet unincorporated Bostonia surrounds and interlocks with city territory here. Brian checks the parcel's jurisdiction before reviewing permits, and he selects comparables based on housing type, age and lot size rather than the community name that happens to appear in listings.

How are converted garages handled for homes along North Mollison?

The appraiser compares the home with assessor and permit records. A permitted conversion of good quality may be included in living area, while an unpermitted one is reported separately and valued based on market reaction. The lack of a garage can also affect value, and that loss is weighed against the extra room gained.
