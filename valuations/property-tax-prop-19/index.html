Property Tax and Prop 19 Appraisal

California property taxes hinge on assessed value, and several situations call for an independent opinion of market value: an assessment appeal after a market dip, a parent-to-child transfer under Proposition 19, or a base-year transfer for an owner who qualifies. Brian Ward's appraisals establish market value as of the date the assessor or appeals board will consider, giving owners and their tax advisors strong evidence to work with.

Prop 19 parent-child transfers

Proposition 19, effective in 2021, narrowed the old parent-child exclusion. A child can keep a parent's taxable value on a family home only by using it as a principal residence, and even in that case the exclusion is limited to one million dollars, inflation-adjusted, above the factored base-year value. Any market value above that threshold gets added to the assessment. That makes the actual market value at the transfer date an important number. Brian provides an independent appraisal of that value. Whether the transfer qualifies, which forms to file, and when to file them are questions for the family's attorney or CPA.

Assessment appeals

When market value drops below the assessed value on January 1, an owner can request a reduction, often called a decline-in-value review. Owners can also appeal a reassessment after a purchase or new construction if they believe the figure is too high. Appeals boards want evidence, and sales comparison is the most persuasive kind for a residence. Brian researches sales around the relevant valuation date and explains why each was chosen. He does not represent owners at hearings or advise on filing deadlines, which vary and should be checked with the county or a tax professional before relying on any date.

Base-year transfers for eligible owners

Under Prop 19, owners 55 or older, those with severe disabilities and victims of certain disasters can also carry their taxable value over to a replacement property located anywhere in California, with adjustments if the new home is more expensive. Both properties' market values matter in that comparison. An independent appraisal of the original or replacement home can support the calculation when the numbers are close. Brian supplies the market value opinion and documents the effective date used. Eligibility, timing rules and the mechanics of the claim are handled by the county assessor's office and the owner's tax advisor, not by the appraiser. Brian keeps his role limited to value.

Matching the assessor's date

Property tax work lives or dies on the effective date. A decline-in-value request looks at January 1. A Prop 19 transfer looks at the date of the transfer or the date of death. A new-construction dispute looks at completion. Brian confirms the exact date before beginning and selects sales that reflect the market on that day. The report explains any adjustment for market change between a comparable's sale date and the effective date, which is a frequent point of pushback from assessors. For a straightforward decline-in-value request a desktop report is sometimes enough, though contested appeals may warrant an inspected report.

In El Cajon and East County

Long-held houses in El Cajon often carry assessed values far below market because of Prop 13, which makes the Prop 19 threshold a real concern when parents pass a home in Fletcher Hills or on Mount Helix to a child. A view lot on Mount Helix can approach or exceed that limit sooner than a valley-floor tract home near Bostonia. Owners filing decline-in-value requests with the San Diego County Assessor also benefit from comparables pulled from the same part of the valley, since hillside and flatland sales behave very differently.

Common questions

Does every parent-to-child transfer need an appraisal?

Not always. If the home's market value is clearly well under the factored base-year value plus the inflation-adjusted one million dollar allowance, the family may not need one. When the value is close to that limit, or when the assessor's estimate seems high, an independent appraisal provides documented support. A CPA or estate attorney can help decide whether one is worthwhile.

Can an appraisal be used at an assessment appeal hearing?

Yes. Appeals boards regularly consider private appraisals as evidence of market value. The report should use the correct valuation date and explain each comparable clearly. Brian prepares the analysis, while owners, their agents or attorneys present the case. Filing deadlines differ by appeal type, so confirm them with the county before ordering.

What if the assessor's value is already lower than market?

Then an appeal may not make sense, and an appraisal could simply confirm that the current assessment is favorable. For Prop 19 transfers the situation differs, since the market value at transfer helps determine how much gets added. A tax advisor can explain how the numbers interact for a specific family before money is spent.
