Brian Ward, the appraiser behind this site

Licensed by California as a Certified Residential appraiser in 2004; more than 7,000 signed reports since then.

Background

Brian Ward became a state-certified residential appraiser in 2004 and has worked continuously as an appraiser since. In two-plus decades he has finished more than 7,000 appraisals, ranging from compact condos in older complexes to backcountry homes on acreage. That volume matters in a county where tract homes, hillside custom houses and rural parcels all share a few square miles. Experience with a wide range of property types gives him a practical sense of how buyers in each segment actually behave, which is exactly what comparable sales are supposed to capture and what a reviewer will test. He works from an office on Paseo Del Sur in the northern part of the city of San Diego.

Why private-party work only

Brian stopped taking financing assignments to concentrate on clients who need a value for their own decisions. Executors, trustees, divorcing spouses, homeowners filing for property tax relief and attorneys preparing for trial all have something in common: the report is read closely by people who were not involved in preparing it. Those readers need reasoning they can follow, not just a number on a form. Working directly for the client also means no intermediary sets the deadline or fee. The person ordering the report talks to the person writing it from start to finish, and questions get answered by the one who did the analysis.

How the reports are written

Each appraisal explains which sales were chosen, why others were left out, and how each adjustment was supported. Photos, a floor plan sketch, maps and permit findings are included so a judge or a family member can see the basis for every conclusion. Brian avoids unexplained jargon and writes in sentences rather than codes. Every report complies with USPAP, the national appraisal standards, and carries his personal signature. When a later question comes up, from a tax preparer, an opposing expert or a co-trustee, he answers it directly and in writing if needed, without extra steps through a middleman.

Knowing El Cajon

El Cajon looks uniform from the freeway, yet value changes block by block. Homes in Fletcher Hills, the streets below Mount Helix and the Granite Hills slopes trade differently from tract houses near Gillespie Field or along Madison Avenue. Brian has spent years working through these distinctions on actual assignments, block by block, including the county pockets of Bostonia and Crest that wrap the city, the condominium complexes off Johnson Avenue, and the older homes near downtown's Main Street. Knowing which sales truly compete with each other is the core skill in a valley this varied, and it is where a generic county-wide approach most often goes wrong.

Beyond the valley

Although El Cajon is the focus here, Brian appraises homes in all of San Diego County's cities and communities, from Oceanside and Fallbrook south to Chula Vista and east to Borrego Springs. Outside the county, he prepares desktop reports for California properties when a local visit is not required, which helps families settling an estate with holdings in several regions. Using one appraiser across multiple properties keeps the method and effective dates consistent, a point that heirs and their attorneys often appreciate when dividing assets fairly. It also means a single conversation covers scheduling, access and billing for the whole group.
